Strategic Finding

Milk sweeps the volume rankings — the top 21 SKUs sold, out of 133 across all dairy products, are all milk — but the highest per-gallon value is in sour cream and dip

Ranked by units sold across every dairy product on the Quantity by Product — All Dairy Products chart, the first non-milk item (QD French Onion Dip, a co-pack SKU) doesn't show up until #22. Milk products are 80.6% of TTM revenue ($15.28M of $18.96M). Two secondary lines are worth watching: Soft Serve Mix at 8.8% ($1.67M, concentrated in just 3 bulk SKUs sold to food service) and Co-Packing at 5.2% ($0.98M). Everything else (Ice Cream, Sour Cream/Chip Dip, Butter, Other) is under 3% of revenue each.

Popular and profitable aren't the same thing here. The two highest-volume milk SKUs, chocolate half-pints and pints, together account for over a quarter of all units sold, but on a price-per-gallon basis they land in the middle of the pack (~$6/gallon), right alongside bulk 2.5-gallon soft-serve mix sold to food service. The real per-gallon standout is sour cream and dip: small 8–16 oz tubs (both CD and QD/Kuster's) dominate the top of the whole SKU list at $14–22/gallon, 3–4x the flagship chocolate milk items and more than 5x plain gallon-jug milk (the cheapest format on the list, ~$2.79–3.19/gallon). That category is tiny today, though (just 0.6% of revenue), so the packaging-economics upside is mostly untapped. Ice cream doesn't show the same small-format premium: its priciest items are two specific 3-gallon bulk flavors (Simply Supercow, Lemon Bar Cheesecake) at $12–13/gallon, closer to a flavor-driven premium than a pack-size effect.

Strategic Finding

Rising prices, not just more head sold, are driving animal revenue growth

Animal revenue grew 58.7% to $1.34M while head sold grew only 16.7% (754 to 880), so most of the gain is price, not volume. Cull cows (Cows Over 2 Years) show the clearest trend: both head sold (46 to 75 per quarter, up 63%) and price ($1,471 to $1,887/head, up 28%) rose every quarter this year, tracking the national cull-cow market's run to record highs. Calves under 5 months, the largest category by volume (515 head), saw prices jump sharply in Jan-Mar '26 to $1,774/head before easing to $1,573, still up 42% from a year ago. Cows 5-24 MOS is the outlier: only 84 head all year, swinging from 5 to 43 head per quarter with no consistent price trend ($2,211 down to $1,593, then back up to $1,987), too small and erratic to read as a signal either way.

Even with that growth, animal sales remain a secondary business line, at roughly 6.7% of total company revenue. But it's growing meaningfully faster than the core milk business, and worth watching as a real, if modest, driver of this year's stronger bottom line.

Production is ahead of target; per-cow output is just under

Annual production is up 4.5% to 4.2M gallons. Strategic Finding

The plant is healthy. The next gains come from running more of it and running higher margin products through it

The plant is turning a solid margin today: $0.91/gallon (18% of revenue) on a TTM basis, holding in the 10–27% range every single month this year. That's on a lean cost structure (labor at $0.49/gal and packaging & ingredients at $1.10/gal against $5.07/gal of revenue).

At 66.7% utilization of the current 108 hrs/wk schedule, there's 36 hrs/wk of open capacity sitting inside today's footprint; the only capital required to use it is additional labor hours. Beyond that, an additional shift would open the schedule to 144 hrs/wk; run at today's same utilization rate, that's roughly 96 hrs/wk of production through the same plant, about a third more volume without adding a square foot.

Strategic Finding

A ~150-mile market, led by Convenience Store/Gas on volume and Supermarket/Grocery on value

56.7% of customers with a mapped distance fall within 100 miles of the plant, and 89.3% fall within 150 miles. Beyond that, penetration falls off sharply (9.7% in the 150–199 mi band, under 1% combined beyond 200 mi).

Convenience Store/Gas is the volume backbone: the most customers of any channel (548+) and the most cases of any true retail channel (226,221), built on high order frequency and route density. Supermarket/Grocery runs the opposite way, with only 108 customers but the highest average cases per account of any retail channel (576): a small number of large, valuable relationships worth actively growing. "Other" (270,063 cases) is the single largest bucket, but almost 80% of that is our Whole Foods order.

Estimated Market Penetration by Distance & Type puts real numbers behind that radius: within 0–49 miles, Country Dairy already reaches an estimated 68% of convenience stores/gas stations and over half of supermarkets, falling off sharply at each band further out, the same pattern as above. Restaurant/Food Service reads near-zero everywhere, but that's mostly a data artifact: the estimate counts every restaurant nationally, including chains that buy through broadline distributors rather than a regional dairy, so it likely understates real whitespace there.

Executive Summary

A profitable year anchored by strong milk quality and efficient plant operations

Revenue grew 8.3% year-over-year, driven by a favorable Q4 milk price environment and expanded half-pint school contracts. The herd averaged 75.2 lbs/cow/day — above the Michigan state average of 71.4 lbs — while a somatic cell count of 187,000 cells/mL kept Country Dairy in the premium quality tier for the full year.

The summer price trough (June–August) remains the primary margin pressure point, compressing monthly operating margin to as low as 6.8% in July. Cost management across plant labor and energy offset much of that seasonal swing. The break-even sits at 72,940 cwt against current production of 94,190 cwt, providing a 22.6% cushion — the strongest buffer in three years.

Strategic Finding

WF gallons and school half-pints are the highest-growth channels — both deserve investment

Country Dairy whole gallons remain the volume anchor at 32% of mix, but water-filtered (WF) gallons are outpacing every other SKU with estimated growth of +12% since FY 2024. This signals a premium-seeking segment in the distributor and specialty retail channel. Half-pint school sales are structurally sticky — these 18,600 cases are weather- and economy-resilient, locked in by institutional contracts.

Consider adding a WF half-pint SKU to target health-premium school contracts where conventional half-pints currently dominate. The Cedar Creek gallon line continues to serve a distinct regional identity segment; consolidating its production run with the CD gallon line on alternating days could reduce changeover downtime by an estimated 8 hours annually.

Strategic Finding

Cull cow quality is improving — capitalize on timing to maximize per-head value

The 13.5% cull rate sits within the healthy 10–15% range, but what has changed is the quality profile of outgoing cows. The average cull cow sold for $890/head in FY 2025 versus $740 in FY 2024 — a 20% increase driven by more cows leaving the herd at peak body condition rather than due to health or reproduction failures. This is a sign of proactive herd management.

Q2 steers captured the peak summer beef market, averaging $1,940/head — the strongest quarterly average on record. For calves, Q1 traditionally sees the highest buyer demand from spring dairies; consider holding male calves born in late December through January for a Q1 sale versus immediately marketing them at birth. Based on FY 2025 price differentials, this timing shift could add an estimated $40–60/head in realized value on 30–40 head annually.

Strategic Finding

Top-quartile yield and premium SCC put Country Dairy in a strong negotiating position

At 75.2 lbs/cow/day, the herd is outperforming the Michigan state average by 5.3% and approaching the top-quartile threshold of 78 lbs. The somatic cell count of 187,000 cells/mL is below the 200,000 threshold required for the highest-tier milk quality premium — and has improved 14,000 cells from FY 2024, suggesting the investment in expanded stall cleaning frequency is paying off.

Butterfat at 3.86% is well above the standard 3.5% component base, providing meaningful revenue uplift. The 2025 spring flush (April–May) added an incremental 730 cwt above the prior year equivalent period. Continuing to invest in summer heat abatement — specifically adding one additional soaker line in the holding pen — is estimated to recover 2–4 lbs/cow/day during July–August and could add 900–1,800 cwt annually at current prices.

Strategic Finding

Plant runs lean on labor and energy — the opportunity is utilization, not cost-cutting

The plant is performing exceptionally well on controllable unit costs: labor at 0.42 hrs/cwt and energy at $1.24/cwt both beat industry averages by more than 10%. Product loss at 1.8% is best-in-class. The gap to close is throughput: at 78% utilization versus a 85% target, there is unused capacity worth an estimated $87,000 in annual revenue if filled without adding headcount or fixed overhead.

Of the 123 annual downtime hours, 81% are planned (cleaning 48 hrs, maintenance 32 hrs, changeovers 24 hrs). The 19 hours of unplanned downtime are modest but worth monitoring. CIP (clean-in-place) protocol optimization and run sequencing to reduce cleaning frequency by batching similar products could recover 6–10 hours annually. The fastest route to 85% utilization is route expansion and institutional sales growth in the school half-pint and 5-gallon foodservice channels.

Strategic Finding

Homegrown feed is Country Dairy's most powerful cost advantage — and it has room to grow

At $7.28/cow/day versus a market-equivalent feed cost of $9.40/cow/day, producing feed on-farm saves $2.12 per cow per day. Across 312 cows and 365 days, that is $241,500 in annual cost avoidance — more than the entire fixed overhead of the processing plant. Both crop yields significantly outperform Michigan averages, with corn silage at 22.4 tons/acre (+13%) and alfalfa at 4.9 tons/acre (+17%).

The highest-leverage crop expansion available is alfalfa acreage. Currently at 95 acres, increasing to 130 acres would reduce annual purchased alfalfa by approximately $28,000 and improve the total mixed ration (TMR) protein profile without feed additive cost. The land base to support this expansion is available on the rented parcels along the east field. Seed and establishment cost would be recovered in approximately 18 months.

Strategic Finding

Fixed cost leverage is Country Dairy's strongest long-term margin lever

At current production of 94,190 cwt, fixed costs allocate to $6.50/cwt. Every additional 1,000 cwt produced with the existing infrastructure reduces that fixed cost per unit by approximately $0.46/cwt — a significant leverage point. This means production growth (whether through herd expansion, higher output per cow, or more processing volume from external milk) has a disproportionate impact on profitability.

Variable costs at $13.00/cwt are well-controlled, with purchased feed ($6.67/cwt) as the dominant line. Each $1/ton reduction in feed cost — achievable through better corn silage fermentation, forage testing to optimize inclusion rates, or the alfalfa expansion discussed in Crop Efficiency — reduces variable cost by approximately $0.08/cwt, or $7,500 annually at current production. The 22.6% buffer above break-even is the widest in recent years and supports modest capital investment in heat abatement or stall improvements.
